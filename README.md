# General Environmental Harvesting of Energy in Humanoid and General Robotics

## Research on Energy Harvesting, Energy Systems, and Self-Sustaining Robotic Platforms

This repository contains my ongoing research on **environmental and motion-based energy harvesting for humanoid and general robotic systems**.

The research investigates how energy that is normally lost during robotic operation, as well as energy available from the surrounding environment, can be captured, converted, stored, and potentially reused to support the robot's power requirements.

The broader goal is to explore how energy harvesting can contribute to the development of more energy-efficient, autonomous, and potentially self-sustaining robotic systems.

> **Status: Ongoing Research**

This is an active research project. The research paper, models, simulations, CAD designs, datasets, and other materials in this repository will continue to be updated as the work progresses.

---

## Research Overview

Modern robotic systems depend heavily on batteries and external power sources. As robots become more autonomous and are deployed for longer periods in environments such as industrial facilities, offshore infrastructure, underwater environments, agriculture, and remote locations, their energy requirements become increasingly important.

At the same time, robotic systems constantly interact with their environment.

A walking robot generates mechanical motion through its joints.  
A UAV interacts with solar radiation and aerodynamic forces.  
An underwater robot operates in an environment containing waves and water movement.  
A mobile robot produces continuous rotational motion through its wheels.

These interactions represent potential sources of energy that could be recovered or harvested.

This research explores the possibility of integrating energy harvesting mechanisms into different classes of robotic systems and evaluating their potential contribution to the robot's overall energy system.

The research considers both:

- **Energy generated by robotic motion**
- **Energy available from the surrounding environment**

The harvested energy will be analysed in terms of its generation potential, conversion efficiency, weight, integration requirements, power conditioning, storage, and effect on the robotic system.

---

# Research Objective

The main objective of this research is to investigate the feasibility of incorporating environmental and motion-based energy harvesting technologies into humanoid and general robotic systems.

The research aims to:

1. Identify potential energy sources available during robotic operation.
2. Analyse the mechanical and environmental energy available for harvesting.
3. Investigate suitable energy harvesting mechanisms for different robotic platforms.
4. Develop mathematical models for the selected harvesting systems.
5. Design conceptual mechanical integration using CAD.
6. Simulate the robotic systems and harvesting mechanisms.
7. Investigate electrical conversion and power conditioning requirements.
8. Analyse energy storage requirements.
9. Compare robotic operation with and without energy harvesting.
10. Evaluate the potential contribution of harvested energy to the robot's overall energy demand.
